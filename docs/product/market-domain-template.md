A market describes demand, suppliers and alternatives around a buying problem. A technical domain organizes related security work. A segment describes a buyer population. A category describes a solution grouping, practice or approach. These working distinctions guide authoring; use the existing repository schema for published records.

## Vendors and products

Reference existing vendor and product IDs separately. Label an illustrative sample and selection method; do not imply exhaustive coverage, ranking or endorsement. Product claims use product-specific evidence. A segment or category overlap alone does not establish direct competition.

## Acceptance review

Complete the [research review record](../market-research/review-template.md) and repository checks.

- Scope is understandable and distinguishable from neighboring records.
- Buyer problems and outcomes are supported or labeled hypotheses.
- Taxonomy and source identities are reused; proposals remain explicit.
- Historical evidence is comparable or its incompatibility is explained.
- Vendor selection and source limitations are visible.
- Relationships, ownership, review dates and correction actions are recorded.
- Human review, checks, merge and publication status are separate.
- UI/catalog changes, if any, have their own acceptance evidence.

Before publication, ask a reader to explain the scope, one buyer problem, one boundary and one evaluation question using the references. Record actual observations; do not claim measured learning gains from completing the template.
