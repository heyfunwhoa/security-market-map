# Control evidence and risk

The risk register, control tests, and audit evidence. GRC platforms track whether a control operated. The control itself lives in identity, AppSec, cloud, or IT.

**Buyer question:** Which controls do we claim, and where is the evidence from the last period?

**Usual owner:** GRC.

**Budget source:** GRC or internal audit.

## Owning seats

- CISO / VP of security (`ciso`)
- GRC or trust lead (`grc-owner`)

## Who vendors sell to

- None recorded.

## Vendors (primary)

- ServiceNow: Workflow platform. Security incident response and GRC are modules buyers may or may not own. The ITSM ticket is not the control.

## What buyers do instead

- Spreadsheets, screenshots, and shared drives for audit evidence. Good enough when: One framework and a single audit a year. Breaks when: Multiple frameworks, continuous monitoring, or audit prep eats the team for weeks. — sourced

## Objections

- "Auditors won't accept tool output as evidence." — hypothesis
- "Security doesn't move revenue." — hypothesis
- "The board needs a simple answer, not more detail." — hypothesis

## Triggers

- Audit
- Control failure
- The company becomes subject to SEC cybersecurity disclosure rules or files its first annual disclosure.
- A New York-regulated financial firm prepares its annual NYDFS Part 500 certification.
- An EU financial entity works through DORA ICT risk-management requirements.
- An audit flags access reviews, privileged access, or evidence gaps.
- The company needs its first SOC 2 or ISO 27001 to close enterprise deals.
- Security questionnaires start blocking or slowing enterprise deals.
- A large funding round sets expectations for enterprise readiness.
