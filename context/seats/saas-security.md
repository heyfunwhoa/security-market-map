# SaaS security

Secure SaaS configuration, grants, and integrations across business-critical apps.

## Typical titles

- ic: SaaS Security Engineer, Business Application Security Engineer
- senior-ic: Senior SaaS Security Engineer, Staff SaaS Security Engineer, Principal SaaS Security Engineer
- manager: SaaS Security Manager, Business Application Security Manager
- director: Director of SaaS Security, Head of SaaS Security

## Owns categories

- SaaS security posture (`sspm`)
- Data loss prevention (`dlp`)
- Workforce identity (`workforce-identity`)
- Data security posture (`dspm`)
- Enterprise browser (`enterprise-browser`)

## Pains

- Can't see or monitor SaaS-to-SaaS tokens and other non-human identities — sourced ([Valence Security](https://www.valencesecurity.com/resources/blogs/valences-latest-research-on-the-2025-2026-state-of-saas-security-overconfidence-despite-critical-gaps))
- Hundreds of SaaS apps configured by business owners — hypothesis
- OAuth grants and integrations nobody reviews — hypothesis

## Objections

- "Business teams own those SaaS apps, not us." Usually about: No authority over app settings or integrations. Reframe to test: Agree on a shared baseline and route fixes to app owners with clear risk, instead of taking over admin. — hypothesis

## Qualification questions

- Does the company run dozens or hundreds of SaaS apps? Good fit if: A large SaaS footprint and many admins. (high weight, hypothesis)
- Are OAuth integrations approved without review? Good fit if: Unknown third-party apps connected to core SaaS. (medium weight, hypothesis)
- Is SaaS limited to one productivity suite? Poor fit if: Few SaaS apps. (low weight, hypothesis)
